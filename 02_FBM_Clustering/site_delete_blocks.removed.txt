    <h3>The scientific arc</h3>
    <div class="method">
      <p style="margin:0 0 10px">The stages answer a chain of questions — each set up by the one before:</p>
      <ul>
        <li><b>Q1 · Can we <i>cluster</i> language?</b> → <b>Not cleanly.</b> Data‑driven clustering gives only weak separation — partly the task design, partly that language isn't one separable process but a distributed, overlapping set. "Find the language cluster" fails by design.</li>
        <li><b>Q2 · So are the clusters <i>random</i>?</b> → <b>No.</b> They track the <b>condition</b> (audio / picture / reading) — a real modality/sensory axis, not noise. (Because clustering keeps conditions as separate samples, the condition contrast is the strongest structure available to find.)</li>
        <li><b>Q3 · Does <i>supervised</i> decoding do better — and which target?</b> → <b>Condition beats network.</b> The <b>condition</b> is readable from a single electrode well above chance; its <b>Yeo network</b> is much harder. The modality signal is strong and decodable; the anatomical‑network signal is faint.</li>
        <li><b>Q4 · <i>Why</i> does condition decode — which features separate the classes?</b> → <b>The early, modality‑specific response.</b> The logistic‑regression weights concentrate in the <b>early stimulus window</b> (before the GO‑cue), where the conditions differ by input (auditory vs visual); the shared speech response afterwards carries little condition information. <span style="color:var(--muted)">(see stage 03)</span></li>
      </ul>
      <p style="margin:10px 0 0"><b>Stage 04</b> then steps back from data‑driven grouping to <b>a‑priori roles</b> and the <b>response‑timing</b> sequence — the "what fires when, and where" that clustering couldn't isolate.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="then"><b>The five figures below answer those four questions in order</b>, one figure per argument. Each gathers the panels belonging to a single claim so it can be judged in one place; the stage tabs hold the same panels with the diagnostics behind them.</div>

<!-- overview / how-the-stages-relate-compare / whole group : describes the 2026-08-03 run and the per-condition track; superseded by the runbook in 02 -->
    <h3>How the stages relate &amp; compare</h3>
    <div class="method"><ul>
      <li><b>Same data, three questions.</b> All three read the identical ERSP substrate; they differ only in <i>who supplies the structure</i> — the data (clustering), the labels (decoding), or prior theory (pooling).</li>
      <li><b>They cross‑check each other.</b> What the unsupervised clustering surfaces on its own should be the same axis the supervised decoder can exploit, and the a‑priori roles should land where both put their signal. Agreement across lenses is the evidence; disagreement flags where the story is fragile.</li>
      <li><b>Different sample units, and the canonical track changed.</b> The clustering run every figure here uses (<code>kmeans/concat_hg 20260803_175417</code>) <i>concatenates</i> the three conditions into one 900‑bin per‑electrode profile — 1027 electrodes — exactly as decoding‑parcellation and pooling do. The older per‑condition track, which kept each electrode × condition as its own sample (2102 samples, K=9), is retained only for comparison. The sample unit still sets what each lens can ask; it is no longer what distinguishes them. <b>The substrate underneath has moved on:</b> the same concatenation on cohort v8 is <b>1680</b> electrodes, and the twelve runs dated <b>20260914&ndash;15</b> are fitted on that; the five figures above still show the 1027-electrode fit.</li>
    </ul></div>

    <h3>Method</h3>
    <div class="method"><ul>
      <li><b>Representations (one orchestrator).</b> raw 129×300 · downsampled 15‑band×30 · minus101 (−1/0/+1) · <b>HFA line</b> (70–150 Hz mean, 1‑D×300). Each → <b>K‑Means</b> + <b>Ward</b>, K‑sweep, best K by silhouette.</li>
      <li><b>Why HFA.</b> Cleanest separation (silhouette HFA 0.152 &gt; downsampled 0.083 &gt; raw 0.045); optimum sits at low K, so HFA uses <code>K∈[6..20]</code>.</li>
      <li><b>Validation.</b> Consensus stability (50 restarts → co‑clustering + per‑cluster Jaccard), gap statistic, anatomical purity (Desikan‑Killiany).</li>
      <li><b>Ranking.</b> Dissolve clusters that fail to bridge both cohorts (EL↔PAT); composite = 4·cross‑patient + 3·stability + 2·condition + 1·spatial‑compactness.</li>
      <li><b>Recon.</b> Each cluster's electrodes rendered on the fsaverage pial surface.</li>
    </ul><div class="runid">source: stage-01 examples</div></div>

<!-- caveats / latebroadband / Why the reading is muscle rather than epilepsy : the muscle reading, superseded by the resolution -->
    <div class="method">
      <b>Why the reading is muscle rather than epilepsy.</b> Two properties, and they point the same
      way:
      <ul>
        <li><b>The frequency shape.</b> Broadband above <b>~50 Hz</b>, flat all the way to
          <b>500 Hz</b>, and <b>suppressed below 30 Hz</b>. An epileptic discharge does the opposite
          &mdash; a spike lifts the low frequencies. A flat high-frequency lift with a low-frequency
          decrease is what EMG looks like on a spectrogram.</li>
        <li><b>The spatial spread.</b> Present in <b>137 of 138</b> channels, on <b>every shaft</b>,
          and in <b>both odd/even split halves</b>. Nothing that uniform across an implant is a focal
          discharge.</li>
        <li><b>It is not one patient's peculiarity.</b> Measured on the audio condition across all
          <b>29</b> patients: EL030 <b>+1.66 dB</b>, EL045 +0.97, EL033 +0.86, PAT_6684 +0.80,
          PAT_3415 +0.77 &mdash; and <b>nine patients show none of it at all</b>. PAT_6684 is fourth on
          that list, not first, which is what rules out reading it as this implant's pathology.</li>
        <li><b>The timing fits the spoken response.</b> It starts at the GO cue and is at its plateau
          through the production half, which is when the patient is speaking. Speech and jaw muscle is
          the most consistent account of all four observations together.</li>
      </ul>
      <b>Stated as what it is.</b> This is an <i>interpretation</i> supported by the frequency shape
      and the spatial spread &mdash; not a proven attribution. No EMG was recorded, so nothing here
      measures muscle directly, and no test has been run that would exclude another broadband
      generator. What can be said without hedging is the measurement: a late, flat, &gt;50 Hz lift with
      low-frequency suppression, everywhere on the implant, in both halves, in five patients strongly
      and in nine not at all.
    </div>


<!-- caveats / latebroadband / Why it matters, and it matters for concat_hg : superseded by the resolution -->
    <p><b>Why it matters, and it matters for <code>concat_hg</code> in particular.</b> The band sits
      inside <b>70&ndash;150 Hz</b>, which is the whole of what <code>concat_hg</code> is built from,
      and on an affected patient it lifts <i>nearly every</i> electrode by a similar late-rising
      amount. So it does not enter the feature matrix as noise on a few contacts &mdash; it enters as a
      patient-wide late component, shared by every electrode of that patient. That is precisely the
      shape a clustering can pick up as structure: a "late response" cluster could be reading the
      speaking rather than the cortex, and electrodes would share it because they share a patient.
      Nothing on this page is corrected for it, and no published number here has been adjusted.</p>


<!-- caveats / latebroadband / What would settle it : settled -->
    <p><b>What would settle it.</b> The per-patient magnitudes are the handle &mdash; the effect is
      measurable per patient on the audio condition and nine patients have none, so a patient-level
      covariate, or a refit with the strongest patients held out, would show whether any cluster
      depends on it. <code>concat_rawds</code> and <code>concat_bands5</code> keep the frequency axis
      and would show the flat-to-500-Hz shape directly, which <code>concat_hg</code> averages away.
      Note also that this interacts with <a href="#gatesplit">the responsiveness gate</a>: a lift on
      nearly every electrode of a patient is a lift on the quantity the gate thresholds.</p>

    <h3>Done 2026-09-08 &rarr; 09</h3>
    <div class="method"><ul>
      <li><b>Per-patient trial rejection in 140.</b> A trial's score is the <b>99th percentile of
        |dB|</b> over its time&ndash;frequency map; two rules, <b>MAD</b> (median + k&middot;1.4826&middot;MAD)
        and the ordinary <b>z</b> (mean + k&middot;SD), OR-ed. A <b>second arm scores the 70&ndash;150 Hz
        band alone</b> (<code>trial_reject_band</code>), because the broadband percentile is set by the
        low frequencies where the power is, so a trial wild only in high gamma barely moves it; the band
        arm is OR-ed with the broadband one and can therefore only reject more. Never more than
        <b>34%</b> of a channel's trials can go. Configured for <b>G-05 only</b>
        (<code>ersp_trial_reject_z</code> 3.0, <code>ersp_trial_reject_hg_mad</code> 3.5) &mdash; every
        other patient has no rejection and reproduces bit-identically. Dropped trials leave the average
        <i>and</i> the odd/even halves. On G-05 the band arm added rejections on <b>35 of 138</b> audio
        channels.</li>
      <li><b>MAD for the band arm, z for the broadband one, on purpose.</b> z takes its mean and SD from
        the sample it is judging, so several bad trials inflate the yardstick and mask each other.
        Synthetic channel, 1/f power, three high-gamma runaways in thirty trials: broadband z=3 rejects
        nothing, band z=3 rejects nothing, <b>band MAD=3.5 rejects exactly the three</b>.</li>
      <li><b>The HG trials figure was hiding most of what it should show.</b> <code>plot_hg_trials</code>
        marked only the trials the ERSP power rule dropped. Three filters run <i>before</i> that rule
        &mdash; wrong answer, stimulus/response duration, duration outlier &mdash; and all three live in
        <code>collect_trials</code>, which returned survivors only, so those trials were never on the
        figure at all. <code>collect_trials</code> now records <b>why</b> each trial went and returns the
        whole table; every excluded trial is drawn at the end, numbered in red with a cross and
        <b>tagged with the filter that took it</b>. Row numbers are now rows of the trial file rather
        than positions in the surviving set, and over-long trials are clipped to the longest kept trial
        so one row cannot squash the picture. For G-05 that is <b>44 of 154</b> trials that used to
        vanish with nothing on the figure to say so &mdash; audio kept 30 of 50, picture 30 of 54,
        reading 45 of 50.</li>
      <li><b>Photodiode: the flip was applied where the figure could not see it.</b> The flip happened
        inside the detector while the QC figure drew the unflipped trace, so the setting looked ignored;
        it is now applied to the array before both. <b>Per-block equalisation</b> was added for a
        photodiode that fades across a session (<code>pd_blocks</code>: <code>"auto"</code> or explicit
        windows), because the detection threshold is a fraction of the sharpest edge in the analysed
        window and so cannot serve a drifting trace. The caveat is in
        <a href="#overview">the stage table</a>: only notebook 13 reaches it.</li>
      <li><b>A latent <code>fmax</code> bug, fixed.</b> Notebooks <b>241</b> and <b>242</b> passed
        <code>fmax=400</code> to the feature builders while <b>240</b> passed 500. <code>fmax</code> is
        not a filter, it is a <i>description</i> of the data: the extractors rebuild the frequency axis
        as <code>linspace(0, fmax, n_freq)</code> and select bins by Hz. The cubes are 129 bins spanning
        0&ndash;500 Hz, so 400 would have made &ldquo;70&ndash;150 Hz&rdquo; select bins truly covering
        <b>89.8&ndash;187.5 Hz</b>. The runs on disk were unaffected &mdash; all twelve
