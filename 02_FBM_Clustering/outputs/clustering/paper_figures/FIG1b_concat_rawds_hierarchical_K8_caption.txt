WHAT THE FIGURE IS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ward's agglomerative clustering (ward linkage, euclidean distance) cut at 8 clusters, 1693 electrodes from 27 patients, in the
15 frequency bands x time feature set (1350 features = 3 conditions x 15 bands x 30 time bins).
Cut at K = 8. Cluster sizes: [385, 65, 129, 85, 74, 147, 179, 629] (largest 37.2% of the cohort).

HOW K WAS CHOSEN
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bi-cross-validation (Owen & Perry 2009): both ROWS (electrodes) and COLUMNS
(features) are held out, so the score can fall as well as rise and the curve can
turn over. A curve that only rises cannot choose a K. Each feature set is scored
in its own home space, so heights are comparable WITHIN a curve and the shape
and the peak are what compare ACROSS curves.

For Ward the held-out block is scored by NEAREST CENTROID (the run's own
predictor) where convex NMF projects it onto its components: the same folds
and the same score, so the curves are comparable in kind, and the values
are read within an algorithm rather than across two.

  feature set        peak K    held-out variance at peak
  concat_hg             30    0.5819
  concat_rawds          30    0.4418   <- this figure
  concat_bands5         30    0.5241
  concat_bands5z        30    0.5335

This figure is cut at K = 8, set on the command line so the three algorithms are read at one K; Ward's own held-out peak for this feature set is K = 30.

PANEL A - held-out variance vs K
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All four feature sets, Ward only. Each point is the mean over the
bi-cross-validation folds; the shaded band on the emphasised curve is +/- 1 SD
across folds. The triangle on each curve marks that curve's own peak. The red
dashed line is the K this figure is cut at (8).
The panel is IDENTICAL in FIG 1a, 1b, 1c and 1d except for which curve is
emphasised, so the four figures can be laid side by side.

PANEL C - how to read a cluster mean
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ABOVE IT, A TRIAL STRIP: what was on the screen, over the time it was there.
Read off the paradigm figure (Figure1_FBM_v1.png). Each condition runs
fixation -> stimulus -> "?", the "?" being the response cue. The stimulus box
spans 0-50% of the block and the "?" box spans 50-100%, so the boundary between
them falls exactly on the dashed GO-cue line.

THE FIXATION SCREEN IS DELIBERATELY ABSENT from the strip. The paradigm has
three screens per trial; these panels show two, because the warp is proportions
(0.0, 0.5, 0.5) and the fixation phase is given NO time bins. It has not been
dropped for tidiness - it occupies no part of the x axis. The baseline every dB
value is expressed against (-0.6 to -0.1 s) does come from it.

The audio, reading and "?" icons are drawn as vectors and stay sharp at any
print size. The picture stimulus is a real line drawing: if a crop of it exists
at stim_picture.png in the paper_figures/assets/ folder it is used, and
otherwise a framed-image glyph stands in, so the figure builds either way.

BELOW THE STRIP, A SCHEMATIC. It is not any real cluster and contains no data - a low-frequency
decrease and a high-frequency increase locked to the GO cue, with amplitude
growing across the three conditions purely so the blocks are distinguishable.
It exists to name the parts: the condition over each block, the GO cue at 50%,
the percent-of-trial axis, and the frequency band edges on y.

PANEL D - does this K generalize?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Computed at EVERY K the run was swept at, not only the published one. For each K,
the electrodes take that K's partition (one column of cluster_labels_by_k.csv)
and two numbers are taken:

  red     the share of CLUSTERS more than 50% of whose electrodes come from
          one patient
  orange  the share of ELECTRODES sitting inside those clusters

They answer different questions and can disagree: two of twenty small clusters
failing is not the same as two of five big ones, and only the second number tells
them apart. The grey curve on the right axis is panel A's held-out variance for
this feature set, on the same x.

WHY BOTH CURVES HAVE TO BE ON ONE PANEL. Held-out variance cannot see this
failure. Splitting a cohort until each patient has their own component fits
held-out data perfectly well - the components are real, they are just components
of individuals rather than of a population. So the held-out peak nominates a K
and this curve says what that K costs; where they disagree is a decision, not a
computation.

  at K = 8:  5 of 8 clusters (62%), holding 32% of the electrodes
  best K on this criterion: 5  (2 of 5 clusters, 18% of electrodes)
  range over K=5..30: 18% to 53% of electrodes in one-patient clusters

PER-CLUSTER PATIENT COMPOSITION at the published K, against a SIZE-MATCHED
RANDOM DRAW - the same number of electrodes sampled without replacement from the
same cohort, averaged over 400 permutations, so the reference inherits the
cohort's own patient imbalance instead of assuming it away. Read it as a CEILING,
not a floor: a real anatomical cluster SHOULD draw on fewer patients than a random
draw, because sEEG coverage is patient-specific. What matters is the other end.

  patients per cluster        3-27  (median 5)   random draw 24.7 of 27
  largest patient's share     12%-91%  (median 60%)   random draw 11%
  clusters with under 5 patients 2 of 8
  WORST: c5 is 91% one patient (134 of its 147 electrodes), drawing on 5 patients.

Written alongside the figure as FIG1b_concat_rawds_hierarchical_K8_patients.csv (per cluster) and
FIG1b_concat_rawds_hierarchical_K8_generalization.csv (per K).

PANEL B, TOP OF EACH BLOCK - the cluster mean
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BLOCK ORDER IS NOT CLUSTER ORDER.  Order used: matched to concat_bands5 (cnmf) by shared electrodes.

Every run labels the SAME 1693 electrodes in the SAME order, so two solutions at
one K can be matched 1:1 by Hungarian assignment, and block position can be made
to mean the same cluster in every figure. concat_bands5 (cnmf)
defines the sequence; its own blocks are ordered by how alike a cluster's three
condition profiles look, most alike first.

THE REFERENCE IS TAKEN AT THIS FIGURE'S OWN K, so both sides of the match have
equal K by construction - which 1:1 assignment requires. It follows that a set of
figures drawn at DIFFERENT K (the held-out peaks: 11, 12, 14, 13) is matched to a
different reference solution in each figure, and their positions are NOT
comparable with one another. Only a set drawn at one shared K is.

CLUSTER IDS, COLOURS AND EVERY NUMBER ARE UNCHANGED. c3 is c3 in panel D, in the
CSVs, in the run report and on the 3-D brain; it just appears somewhere else on
this page. Renumbering to match the page would have broken that correspondence.

THE ORDER IS A SORT KEY, NOT A RESULT. Correlation is scale-free, so two
conditions with the same shape and very different amplitude score high. That is
what "looks the same" means for a shape compared by eye, and it is not what it
would mean if this were reported as a measure of modality invariance. Nothing is
computed from it and nothing is claimed by it.

  order drawn   c6 c3 c2 c0 c7 c4 c1 c5
  similarity    +0.81 +0.90 +0.90 +0.93 +0.82 +0.43 +0.83 +0.52
  ref cluster    2  4  6  3  5  1  0  7
  jaccard       0.24 0.29 0.50 0.27 0.18 0.35 0.00 0.58

  matched on shared electrodes; the Jaccard above is always on the HARD labels, so
  the quality number means one thing whichever basis was used.
  weakest match: c1 at Jaccard 0.00   median 0.28

  A 1:1 assignment ALWAYS returns a partner for every cluster, whether or not
  one exists. A low Jaccard is a cluster that has no real counterpart and was
  paired anyway; position implies a correspondence that the number denies.
  The matched ids and these overlaps are deliberately NOT drawn on the figure
  - which clusters agree, and which are left out, is Figure 2's subject.
  Per-cluster values are written alongside as ..._matching.csv.

Every cluster gets ONE BLOCK, three rows deep: its mean on top; underneath, the
two hemisphere renders of the electrodes that carry it; and under those, WHO THE
CLUSTER IS MADE OF - one horizontal bar, one colour-coded segment per patient,
width proportional to that patient's electrodes in the cluster, widest first. A
cluster that is one patient's electrode strip is a single block of colour there;
a cluster drawn from the cohort is a fine stripe. Patient colours come from
tab20 + tab20b and are stable across the whole figure, so a patient is the same
colour in every cluster - but they are NOT stable across figures 1a-1d, because
each figure sorts the patients present in its own run.

The mean spans the full block width - twice a single render - so both fit under
it. A cluster whose largest patient is over 50% is titled in red, with that share.

The mean is drawn in the representation the feature set was actually clustered
in. Each panel holds the three conditions (audio, picture, reading (sentences)) concatenated left to right,
separated by a solid line. Each condition is time-normalised to 30 bins spanning
0-100% of its own trial, and the GO cue is at 50% of every block (the dashed
line) because the warp uses proportions (0.0, 0.5, 0.5). THERE IS NO ABSOLUTE
TIME AXIS: trials differ in length and were warped to a common proportion, so
the axis is percent-of-trial and not seconds.

The band sets are drawn as FREQUENCY x TIME. Rows are the 15 bands, lowest at the
bottom: 1-4Hz, 4-8Hz, 8-13Hz, 13-20Hz, 20-30Hz, 30-50Hz, 50-70Hz, 70-100Hz, 100-130Hz, 130-170Hz, 170-220Hz, 220-270Hz, 270-320Hz, 320-360Hz, 360-400Hz.
Colour is dB vs baseline, on one diverging scale shared by all 8 clusters,
symmetric about zero at +/- 2.578 (the 99th percentile of |cluster
mean| over every cluster, band and time bin). Blue is a decrease, red an
increase.

DISPERSION ACROSS ELECTRODES is shown on every cluster mean, and it is the
same quantity in both representations: +/- 1 STANDARD DEVIATION across the
electrodes in the cluster, at each point.

  concat_hg     a shaded band at mean +/- 1 SD
  the band sets an OUTLINE enclosing the time-frequency bins where |mean| > SD,
                i.e. exactly the bins where that band would not span zero

The outline is drawn on CELL EDGES, never between them. A contour would
interpolate between band centres and imply a resolution a 5- or 15-band grid
does not have - the reason lf_centroids.render_heatmap_centroid uses SD dots
instead of contours. Condition blocks are treated as non-adjacent, so a region
cannot appear to run from the end of one trial into the start of the next.

The block title carries the share of bins inside the outline: "agree NN%".

  agreement per cluster   0% - 34%  (median 17%)
  tightest c2 at 34%   loosest c7 at 0%

WHAT AGREEMENT IS NOT. It is not a test: there is no null, and no correction
over the 1350 bins it is evaluated on per cluster. And it is NOT
evidence that a cluster generalises - the opposite, if anything. Electrodes on
one patient's electrode strip are highly correlated, so a cluster that is mostly
one person will tend to show MORE agreement, not less. Agreement asks whether
the members look like their own centroid; panel D asks whether the members are
more than one person. A cluster can score well here and still be one patient.
A singleton cluster is given NO agreement rather than total agreement, because
there is no dispersion evidence in a sample of one.

Cluster colours are the visualizer's own palette - hue = 360*j/K, s = 0.62,
l = 0.52 - so a cluster is the same colour here, in the run report and on the
3-D brain.

PANEL B, BOTTOM OF EACH BLOCK - where those electrodes are
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two 3-D surface renders per cluster, on the fsaverage pial surface, produced by
pyvista/VTK offscreen with the same material constants the coverage and cluster
renders use - read live from functions/lf_recon_shared_config.py, not copied, so
these figures cannot drift away from every other render in the project.

THE LEFT HEMISPHERE IS SEEN FROM THE LEFT AND THE RIGHT FROM THE RIGHT, so each
is a true lateral view: anterior falls on the LEFT of the left render and on the
RIGHT of the right one. Drawing both from the same side, as an earlier version
did, shows one hemisphere from inside the head. Each render holds only that
hemisphere's electrodes; an electrode never appears in both.

  electrodes with fsaverage coordinates   1625 / 1693
  left render                             768
  right render                            857

COLOUR AND SIZE, THE VISUALIZER'S OWN RULE. This is transcribed from
clustering_visualizer.html's updateElectrodes(), not invented here, so these
renders and the brains on the site read the same way.

THIS IS A HARD PARTITION, so w is 1 for the electrodes in the cluster and 0 for
every other electrode: members draw in full cluster colour, at full radius and
fully opaque; the rest draw grey 148 at the smallest radius and 0.10
opacity. The rule below is the one the convex-NMF figures use, evaluated at
those two values, so the brains of the three algorithms read alike.

      colour: FULL cluster colour at a loading of 0.50 or above; below
              that, mixed toward grey 148 and reaching it exactly at a flat 1/K
              mixture -   g = clip((w - 1/K) / (0.50 - 1/K), 0, 1)
      size:   t = clip((w - 1/K) / (0.70 - 1/K), 0, 1)
              radius = 1.5 * (1.02 + 1.20 * t) mm
      opacity: 0.10 at a loading of zero, rising linearly to fully
              opaque at 0.70 -   a = 0.10 + 0.90 * clip(w / 0.70, 0, 1)
              measured from ZERO, not from 1/K, unlike the other two: this
              channel exists to sink the uninvolved population into the
              background, and a floor at 1/K would leave everything below a flat
              mixture invisible and everything just above it nearly solid

where w is 1 for the electrodes in this cluster and 0 for every other electrode
(a hard partition has no graded loading). THREE CHANNELS FOR THREE QUESTIONS:
colour says whether the electrode is expressing this component at all, size says
how strongly, opacity sinks the rest of the cohort into the background. An
earlier version ramped colour across the whole range instead, so a typical
loading - the median is around a third - landed a third of the way out of grey
and every render came out muddy.

EVERY ELECTRODE OF THE HEMISPHERE IS DRAWN BY THIS ONE RULE, applied to its own
loading on this cluster. There is no separate background layer and no special
case for the argmax winner: an electrode's appearance says what it expresses,
not which column happened to be largest. Order-independent transparency (VTK
depth peeling) is enabled, or a faint sphere in front of a solid one would erase
it depending only on draw order.

Both are RENDERING choices. They change how a loading is drawn, never the
loading, the label, or any number reported anywhere.

  every one of the 1693 electrodes draws at w = 1 in its own cluster and at
  w = 0 in the other 7: full colour, full radius and opaque there;
  grey, smallest and faint everywhere else.

WHAT THIS FIGURE DOES NOT SHOW
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - No statistical test of the clustering. Separation against a matched null,
    anatomical coherence and leave-one-patient-out are reported separately, and
    are valid at ONE K only because each is scored against a null refitted at
    that K. At the time of writing they exist for concat_hg and concat_rawds
    only; concat_bands5 and concat_bands5z have no statistics at any K.
  - Panel D and the patient bars are about PATIENT COMPOSITION, which is
    necessary for a cluster to be a population response type and is not
    sufficient. A cluster can draw on every patient and still not replicate.
  - The 50% line in panel D is a convention, not a test. It is not
    calibrated against a null; it is a threshold chosen to be readable. So is
    the 1-SD line the agreement outline draws.
  - No comparison against convex NMF, k-means or archetypal analysis within this
    figure; FIG 2 is where the algorithms are compared. Panel A is Ward only.
  - The renders collapse depth: an electrode deep in the temporal lobe and one
    on the lateral surface can overlap in the image.
  - The label is a HARD assignment: it says which cluster an electrode is in,
    not how close it came to another. The run's per-electrode silhouette
    (labels.csv) is not drawn.
