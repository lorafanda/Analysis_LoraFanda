WHY A SPIN NULL
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FIG 3 tests each cluster against a shaft-shift null that rolls the cluster labels
along each shaft and leaves the atlas in place. Clusters and shafts live at the
same spatial scale, so for a type that fills whole shafts the roll changes nothing
and the null reproduces the observed share almost exactly - it has no power. The
spin test (Alexander-Bloch et al. 2018, NeuroImage 178:540) turns it round: the
electrodes, their shafts, patients and labels stay exactly where they are, and the
MAP is randomised. Each electrode is anchored to its nearest fsaverage pial vertex;
that vertex's point on the fsaverage sphere is rotated by a random rotation (drawn
uniformly; on the right hemisphere the mirror of the same rotation, so the two
sides stay symmetric), the rotated point is read back to the nearest sphere vertex,
and the LanA volume is sampled at that vertex's pial coordinate with the same
sampler that gave the contacts their values (nearest 2 mm voxel after the
MNI305 -> MNI152 affine; lf_atlas_corr.sample_atlas_at_contacts). The spun map
keeps LanA's smoothness, size and left-right asymmetry; what it breaks is only the
alignment between the map and the electrodes. Observed values are the atlas at the
anchor vertices, so observed and spun are on the same footing (a surface point).

The second thing FIG 3 cannot show is the inverse question: what does the LanA
network itself DO during the task. Panels A and B are the mean response (the run's
own features, dB re baseline, band x time x condition) of the electrodes inside the
network at P >= 0.1 against the electrodes outside it, and their difference;
the outlined cells are where the difference is beyond what the spun maps give
(two-sided, Benjamini-Hochberg over every cell). Panel E is the one-number version:
the RMS of that difference over all cells, observed against spun.

Everything is computed twice: on all electrodes and on the LEFT hemisphere alone,
because LanA is left-lateralised and the cohort is right-heavy, so a type that
leans left is 'inside the network' for that reason alone unless the hemisphere is
held fixed.

HOW TO READ IT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A cluster whose bar sits inside its grey box is where a random map with LanA's statistics would put
it: its share of language cortex is what its shafts' positions give any map of that size and
smoothness. A bar above the box is a type that sits in language cortex more than such a map
predicts; below, less. The outlined cells of A and B are the parts of the network's response that no
spun map reproduces: that is the response the LanA network has and the rest of the sampled cortex
does not, in this task. Read B (left only) before A: on all electrodes a difference can be the two
hemispheres differing.

WHAT THIS FIGURE DOES NOT SHOW
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Not evidence of language responsiveness per se: LanA is the sentences-versus-
    nonwords contrast, which subtracts auditory, visual-form and articulatory cortex.
    Types built on those phases are outside the network by construction.
  - The spin keeps the map's statistics, not the cohort's: where no patient has a
    shaft, no spun map can put an electrode either.
  - Thresholds are a declared choice (0.1, 0.2); the
    cell test of A and B uses the first.
